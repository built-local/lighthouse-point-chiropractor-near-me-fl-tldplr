How Spinal Decompression Works

During a decompression session, you lie on a specialized table that gently extends and releases in controlled cycles. This creates negative pressure within the spinal discs, which can help draw bulging material back inward and reduce nerve compression. Over a series of treatments, many patients experience gradual improvement in pain, numbness, or weakness that stems from disc problems.

Decompression works best when combined with other care—chiropractic adjustments, therapeutic massage, or therapeutic exercises—to address both the mechanical issue and the surrounding muscles and ligaments.

Is Spinal Decompression Right for You?

The effectiveness of decompression depends on your underlying condition. Bulging or herniated discs often respond well. Severe structural damage or spinal instability may require a different approach. Dr. McNamara-Krauss will tell you honestly whether she believes decompression can help you avoid or delay more invasive options.

A thorough exam—including your medical history, physical assessment, and sometimes imaging—determines whether this treatment makes sense for your situation.
